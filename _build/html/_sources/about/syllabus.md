# Syllabus
## Course Content
This module includes the following topics:
- Introduction to geoinformation;
- Spatio-temporal representations and databases;
- Spatial data analysis;
- Cartographic visualization techniques;
- Open geospatial data acquisition and integration.  

As well as,
- Introduction to GIS software (e.g., ArcGIS Pro, QGIS) components and interface;
- Basic GIS operations, including selecting, editing, and modifying features, and symbolization;
- Advanced GIS operations, including vertical and hydrological analysis, network analysis, and other spatial analysis, 2D and 3D cartographic visualization, map design, and advanced applications of GIS;
- Field data acquisition and open geospatial data retrieval.
## Teaching and Learning Methods
The module is structured in **Lectures with integrated Exercises**.
The Lectures provide the theoretical foundations of geoinformation. They impart knowledge about spatiotemporal data representation and management, spatial data analysis, and cartographic techniques for visualizing spatial data. 
The integrated Exercise part of this module allows the students to implement the concepts of geoinformation into practice. An introduction to desktop GIS software (ArcGIS Pro or QGIS) will be provided. Students will analyze and visualize geodata using a range of analytical tools and visualization techniques. The Exercises are carried out in a computer lab individually, partly under supervision within contact hours, and partly in self-study. Some of the Exercises require students to submit assignments. By completing these tasks, students can verify their achievement of the intended learning outcomes and will receive feedback from the instructor.
## Learning Objectives
After the successful conclusion of the module, the students are able to:
- Describe the core concepts, scope, and challenges of geoinformatics, including different perspectives on space and time.
- Explain spatial and temporal data models, and compare different data structures and indexing methods for managing geoinformation.
- Evaluate the quality of spatial data and analyze potential pitfalls in spatial data analysis.
- Apply appropriate spatial analysis methods to solve geographic problems.
- Integrate heterogeneous geospatial data into a GIS using harmonization and transformation techniques.
- Implement map generalization algorithms to simplify spatial data while preserving essential patterns and relationships.
- Design effective cartographic products using principles of symbolization, generalization, and layout.
- Operate desktop GIS software (e.g., ArcGIS Pro, QGIS) to perform data editing, spatial analysis, and visualization tasks.
## Description of Examination Method
A **written exam of 60 minutes** takes place at the end of the semester. No auxiliary materials are permitted during the exam. By answering the questions, students demonstrate that they have acquired the required knowledge in spatiotemporal data representation and management, spatial data analysis, and cartographic techniques for visualizing spatial data.
The exam includes tasks in which students must provide valid definitions, explain key concepts, theoretically implement and evaluate case studies, and address design challenges. All intended learning outcomes are assessed in this written exam.
An **optional bonus** (an improvement of one intermediate grade level, i.e., 0.3 better) is awarded for a passed module examination (note: a 4.3 is not upgraded to 4.0) if the student **successfully completes all required Exercise assignments**. Each assignment must be completed independently, properly documented, uploaded to the Moodle e-learning platform, and confirmed as passed by the lecturer.
